About me

Mostly Python, pointed at machine learning.

I'm a Computing student at Queen's University in Kingston, Ontario, Canada, on the Arts and Science Scholarship. The work I care about is where a model has to deal with messy real-world signal rather than a clean benchmark dataset.

I write mostly Python. It's where I'm quickest and where nearly all of my machine-learning work lives. JavaScript and SQL are the other two I use regularly, mainly for the web apps below.

The direction I want to go is AI and machine learning, and specifically applied computer vision. I've now done one real research project end to end, and it confirmed the thing I suspected: the hard part is rarely the model. It's getting usable signal out of imperfect input, deciding what's actually worth measuring, and not needing a labelled dataset you can't afford to produce.

That project came out of archery. I shot competitively from 2021, including for Canada at a couple of international events, and I coach now. That means a lot of evenings watching other people's form on video and telling them what to change. Most of what a coach does at that level is pattern recognition over repeated motion, which is a machine-learning problem wearing a tracksuit.

So I worked on STRAIGHT with two researchers at Boston University. It reads a practice clip, detects the shot phase, estimates pose, and scores each shot against the archer's own prior attempts rather than a textbook ideal. It was accepted as a poster at ACM UIST.

Next I want more ML depth and more people to build it with: bigger datasets, harder problems, and a team rather than a solo repo.

I'm looking for software engineering internships and research positions, particularly anything touching computer vision, applied ML, or human-computer interaction.
