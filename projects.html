STRAIGHT2025

Archery form analysis from video pose estimation

Problem
Consistent form depends on reproducing timing and alignment across every shot, but a coach can only watch a fraction of them. The unobserved shots are exactly where inconsistency hides.
What I built
A pipeline that takes a raw practice clip, detects the shot phase, estimates pose, and scores timing, alignment and stability against the archer's own prior attempts. An LLM converts the measurements into coaching-style feedback with a drill attached.
Hardest part
Segmenting without labels. Hand-labelling phase boundaries across 733 frames per clip doesn't scale, so pose heuristics narrow the window and binary-search VLM queries locate the boundary in about log₂(n) queries. That's what holds 98% phase detection on low-resolution footage and with dummy training aids.
My part
The segmentation pipeline and the feature-selection pass that cut 100+ pose features to the 15 with real predictive signal, plus the random-forest phase model at 87% accuracy within 10 frames. Co-authored with two researchers at Boston University.

QSTUDY2026

Study-group matching for university students

Problem
First-year students at a large university have no way to find people in the same courses who actually want to study together.
What I built
A Flask backend and React frontend deployed as two separate services against managed Postgres. Session-based authentication, a REST API over a schema for accounts, groups and membership, and live filtering so a student finds a relevant group without reading a list.
Hardest part
The failures were operational, not algorithmic. Cookies worked locally and silently broke cross-site in production. The fix required understanding what SameSite=None actually costs and why a CSRF defence becomes mandatory once you set it. Separately, a schema change never reached the live database, because create_all() only creates tables it can't find; it will never alter one that already exists.

SWIPEHIRE2025

Resume review, turned into a swipe deck

Problem
Reviewing resumes one PDF at a time is tedious, and tedium is where attention goes to die. My CS50 final project: make the decision itself feel like a game. Swipe, not scroll.
What I built
A swiping review interface over an extracted-text queue. PyPDF2 pulls the resume text on upload, so the reviewer sees a normalised card instead of a PDF viewer, and every decision persists against the session.
Hardest part
The auth layer. Parameterised queries and server-side session handling rather than string-built SQL: the difference between a login form and a SQL injection vector.
